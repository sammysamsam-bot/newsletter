### Antisemitic assaults in the US climb to a 46-year high

#### The violence, in numbers
- Physical assaults against Jewish Americans rose 4% to 203 incidents in 2025, the highest count since 1979.
- Thirty-two of those assaults involved deadly weapons, a 39% jump over the 23 recorded in 2024.
- Fatalities returned for the first time since 2022; two Israeli Embassy staffers were shot dead outside the Capital Jewish Museum in Washington, DC.

#### Where the danger concentrated
- New York state accounted for 44% of all documented physical attacks against Jews.
- A Colorado man firebombed a community event held to spotlight Israeli hostages, wounding an 82-year-old attendee.
- Overall antisemitic incidents dropped 33% to 6,274, but the decline came entirely from harassment and vandalism, not violence.

#### Context
The ADL’s annual audit shows a decoupling of overall incident volume from physical severity. The weaponization trend — and the return of fatalities — suggests a minority of offenders escalating beyond the social-media hostility that drives the headline numbers. Whether 2026 sustains the pattern as campaign rhetoric intensifies is an open, unsettling question.

### North Korea erases unification from its constitution

#### The revised text
- The constitution now defines national territory as the land bordered by China, Russia, and South Korea to the south, abandoning any claim to the whole peninsula.
- All references to reunification have been deleted, codifying Kim Jong Un’s “two hostile states” doctrine.
- For the first time, the constitution explicitly grants Kim authority over the country’s nuclear weapons.

#### The strategic pivot
- Experts view the change as permanent rather than a bargaining chip; one Oxford scholar said Pyongyang no longer sees the Koreas as part of one nation.
- The preamble omits older ideological language and, notably, does not use strongly hostile terms toward Seoul despite labeling it the “principal foe.”
- The shift formalizes rhetoric that has built since late 2023, when Kim declared reunification impossible.

#### Context
This is legal architecture, not a speech. Anchoring the nuclear command in the supreme leader’s constitutional role ties permanent separation to the deterrent. The immediate test is whether Seoul and Washington treat it as irreversible fact or as another round of performative brinkmanship — the military posture on the DMZ will tell.

### Food stamp work rules aren’t delivering the promised employment lift

#### The research finding
- A new study concludes that adding work requirements to SNAP does not measurably increase employment among recipients.
- Researchers point to unstable schedules, untreated health conditions, and lack of childcare as reasons mandates fail to raise labor-force participation.
- The finding arrives after the One Big Beautiful Bill Act tightened work rules, helping push 3.5 million people off the rolls.

#### The administration’s counter
- The White House and allied commentators frame the 3.5 million roll reduction as a fiscal success that restores self-sufficiency.
- An editorial pushing this line argues the reforms cut bloat and shrink deficits, calling the changes a model for future administrations.

#### Context
The gap here is between “fewer people on aid” as a metric and “more people working” as an outcome. The study suggests the policy’s main engine is exclusion, not economic mobility. Poverty and food-insecurity data over the next year will arbitrate which story holds — and both sides are already using these numbers to frame midterm arguments.
