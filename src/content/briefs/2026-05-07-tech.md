> **Summary.** SpaceX's prospective public offering reportedly requires shareholders to waive rights to sue the company or its executives, while concentrating voting power with Musk through a non-standard share structure. The terms would make SpaceX among the most governance-hostile large-cap listings in recent memory. Panthalassa *(floating data center startup, $200M raised)* and others are watching how retail investors receive these constraints.

The reporting here rests on a single Financial Times source, but the framing aligns with Musk's documented preference for investor control minimization at Tesla and X. The waiver-of-suit clause is particularly aggressive—most public companies expose themselves to derivative shareholder actions even with dual-class structures. What's unclear is regulatory tolerance: SEC staff have historically pushed back on litigation waivers as contrary to public policy, though post-2024 enforcement appetite is an open question. The article doesn't address whether institutional investors—typically the price-setters in IPOs—would accept these terms at expected valuation levels.

Musk's concentration of voting power matters because SpaceX's Starship and Starlink operations involve national security contracts and FAA licensing. A private-equity-style governance structure at public scale creates friction with the transparency expectations embedded in those relationships. We don't know if this is a negotiating position or final terms. The signal worth tracking is whether lead underwriters accept the structure; their silence so far suggests they're testing appetite rather than committing.
