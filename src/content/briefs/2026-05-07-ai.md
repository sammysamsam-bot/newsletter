### OpenAI ships five, with two worth your attention

> **Summary.** GPT-5.5 Instant *(incremental upgrade to ChatGPT's default model)* rolls out with accuracy and personalization improvements; MRC *(Multipath Reliable Connection, new networking protocol for AI training clusters)* is open-sourced through OCP *(Open Compute Project)* for large-scale training resilience. The rest—Uber integration, Singular Bank case study, B2B Signals research—are marketing collateral and partnership announcements.

The GPT-5.5 Instant release is modest in framing but telling in what OpenAI *isn't* saying. No benchmark tables, no GPT-4.5-style "test-time compute" architecture reveal—just "smarter, clearer, more personalized" with a system card promising reduced hallucinations. This reads as a maintenance upgrade to the consumer product line, not a frontier advance. The model itself may be fine; the communication strategy signals OpenAI has stopped treating every shipping cadence as a capital-E Event. That's healthy discipline, or it's deprioritization of the consumer tier. We won't know which until we see if GPT-5 proper arrives before year's end.

MRC is more genuinely interesting—OpenAI's own networking stack for training clusters, now contributed to OCP. Training supercomputers spend meaningful wall-clock time waiting on network congestion; reliable multipath routing matters for utilization rates and for anyone building at similar scale. The release format (open spec, not open source) suggests OpenAI wants this adopted as infrastructure standard, not maintained as competitive moat. What we don't have: independent benchmarks against existing RDMA *(Remote Direct Memory Access)* or NCCL *(NVIDIA Collective Communications Library)* configurations. The claimed improvements are plausible given the problem domain, but "released via OCP" is not the same as "deployed and validated outside OpenAI."

([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-5-instant) · [MRC announcement](https://openai.com/index/mrc-supercomputer-networking))

### DeepSeek's first raise could value it at $45B—if it happens

> **Summary.** DeepSeek *(Chinese AI lab, emerged early 2025 with efficient training techniques)* reportedly seeks initial external funding at a valuation rivaling Databricks; the round would mark its first outside investment after state-linked origins.

The $45B figure is sourced to "people familiar with the matter," which means DeepSeek's principals or their bankers are floating it. DeepSeek's technical credibility is real—the R1 model demonstrated that efficiency gains could substitute for brute compute, a finding that genuinely rattled U.S. labs' planning assumptions. But valuation is about revenue and defensibility, not architecture papers. DeepSeek has no disclosed enterprise business, no API revenue figures, and operates under export control constraints that limit its compute access. The reported valuation may be aspirational benchmarking against Moonshot AI and similar Chinese labs, or it may reflect state-affiliated capital willing to pay strategic premiums. Either way, this is a financing story until the round closes and terms leak.

([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/06/deepseek-could-hit-45b-valuation-from-its-first-investment-round/))

### Genesis AI demos full-stack robotics; evidence remains investor-grade

> **Summary.** Genesis AI *(Khosla-backed robotics startup, $105M seed)* debuted GENE-26.5 *(foundational model for general-purpose robotics)* alongside dexterous-hand manipulation demos, claiming vertical integration from model to hardware.

The demo video shows impressive hand coordination—peg insertion, tool use, adaptive grasping. What's absent is any third-party reproduction, any customer deployment, any benchmark against Figure AI or Tesla Optimus on standardized tasks. Genesis is making the right investor-facing moves: impressive video, "full stack" narrative, Vinod Khosla's credibility as signal. The technical claim is that a single foundation model can generalize across robotic embodiments without task-specific fine-tuning. If true, that's architecture shift. If partially true in staged conditions, that's robotics circa 2026. The honest read: we don't know yet, and Genesis has incentive to keep it that way until Series A closes.

([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/06/khosla-backed-robotics-startup-genesis-ai-has-gone-full-stack-demo-shows/))

### xAI's real business may be infrastructure, not intelligence

> **Summary.** TechCrunch argues xAI *(Elon Musk's AI company, originally model-focused)* has pivoted toward data center construction and power contracting, effectively becoming a "neocloud" *(cloud provider built for AI workloads, distinct from hyperscale incumbents)*.

The evidence is circumstantial but plausible: xAI's Memphis cluster is reportedly 1.2 GW with more land acquired, Musk's public statements increasingly emphasize power and silicon, and the Grok chatbot itself is barely mentioned in recent communications. The reframe fits a pattern—Musk ventures often start with aspirational product narratives and converge on infrastructure plays (Starlink, Tesla Supercharger, now possibly xAI). What's less certain is whether this constitutes a deliberate pivot or opportunistic expansion. Training frontier models still requires that infrastructure; owning it doesn't preclude model development. The "neocloud" frame may be more descriptive of xAI's current capabilities than of strategic intent. Still, if accurate, it suggests Musk has concluded model commoditization is coming fast and durable advantage lies in electrons and acres.

([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/06/is-xai-a-neocloud-now/))

### Snap kills $400M Perplexity deal

> **Summary.** Snap *(social media company)* confirmed its November 2024 agreement with Perplexity *(AI search startup)* ended without integration, despite reported $400M value.

"Amicably ended" is corporate euphemism for something went wrong. The deal structure—reportedly $400M over multiple years, with Perplexity powering Snapchat's AI search—would have been Perplexity's largest distribution partnership and a validation of its consumer reach. Snap's AI search now runs on unspecified "other providers." The cancellation matters for Perplexity's growth story: its $500M raise at $14B valuation assumed distribution deals could substitute for organic user acquisition. Losing Snap suggests either Perplexity couldn't meet technical requirements at scale, or Snap found cheaper equivalent capability elsewhere, or both parties discovered the economics didn't work. Perplexity's core product remains strong for research-heavy users; its platform strategy just took visible damage.

([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/06/snap-says-its-400m-deal-with-perplexity-amicably-ended/))
