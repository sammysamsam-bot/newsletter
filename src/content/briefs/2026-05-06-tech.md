### SpaceX’s IPO Terms Bar Investor Lawsuits and Lock In Musk’s Absolute Control

#### Inside the S-1

- Buyers must waive the right to sue SpaceX, its officers, or Elon Musk for nearly any reason, including fiduciary breaches.
- Musk holds high-vote stock that guarantees he can block any board challenge regardless of his economic stake.
- The filing concedes that Musk’s other ventures — Tesla, X, xAI — create permanent conflicts of interest with SpaceX.

#### What This Means for Capital Markets

- This is the first IPO by a major U.S. company structured so investors cannot pursue standard securities-law protections.
- The registration uses a Delaware Public Benefit Corporation form, which permits overriding shareholder value for founder-defined social purposes.
- Nasdaq and the SEC have so far not signaled they will block the listing despite the liability waiver being unprecedented in scale.

#### Context

The S-1 makes explicit what Musk’s private companies have long practiced: concentrated control is not a bug, it’s the product. If the offering clears the SEC without challenge, it rewrites the floor for what public-market investors can demand from founders. Watch whether institutional allocators quietly fall in line or balk at a vehicle that strips their last resort right.

### Microsoft’s AI Data Center Boom Is Undermining Its Own Climate Pledges

#### The Carbon Math

- Microsoft’s emissions rose 30% between 2020 and 2024, driven almost entirely by data center construction and supply-chain manufacturing.
- The company has signed new power agreements that include natural gas peaker plants in regions where grids cannot supply enough renewables.
- Its carbon-negative-by-2030 target assumed a slower AI build cycle and more abundant clean power, neither of which materialized.

#### The Policy Tension

- Microsoft is lobbying for faster permitting of transmission lines while simultaneously taking whatever baseload power it can secure today.
- Competitors’ similar build rates mean the total grid burden is competitive, not just additive — there is a finite pool of green electrons.

#### Context

This is not a Microsoft problem alone; every hyperscaler’s net-zero roadmap was written pre-ChatGPT. What’s new is the candour leaking from internal planning documents, which treat the gap between commitment and reality as a scheduling problem rather than a philosophical one. The test is whether the next sustainability report uses accounting offsets or admits the timeline has slipped.

### Google’s Gemma 4 Open Models Claim a 3x Speedup With No Quality Degradation

#### The Technique

- Gemma 4 employs speculative decoding: a lightweight draft model predicts multiple future tokens, which the main model then verifies or rejects in parallel.
- Google says this shifts the bottleneck from compute to memory bandwidth, yielding up to threefold throughput gains across a range of batch sizes.
- The speedup applies to both cloud-hosted and local deployments, with the draft model bundled as part of the open release.

#### What’s Still Under Examination

- Google reports no loss in benchmark scores, but speculative decoding’s gains are task-sensitive — long-form generation benefits more than tightly constrained reasoning.
- The claimed 3x is an upper bound measured under favourable batch conditions; real-world developer throughput will land lower.

#### Context

Efficient inference for open models matters because it lowers the cost of running capable AI outside proprietary clouds — exactly the axis on which the open-vs-closed debate turns. Independent benchmarking that tests latency, throughput, and output distribution drift across varied workloads will tell us whether this is an engineering advance or a carefully staged marketing number.
