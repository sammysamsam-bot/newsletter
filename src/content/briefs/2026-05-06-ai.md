### OpenAI shipped a stack of things in 24 hours

GPT-5.5 Instant became ChatGPT's new default with claimed gains in accuracy, reduced hallucinations, and expanded personalization controls — the personalization piece is the interesting frontier, and the system card was published alongside per their improving safety hygiene. They also launched a self-serve ChatGPT Ads Manager with cost-per-click bidding and "privacy-preserving" measurement, opened the MRC supercomputer networking protocol via the Open Compute Project, and dropped a B2B Signals research piece framing enterprise adoption via agentic Codex workflows. Read together: default-model upgrade plus monetization plus infra openness — OpenAI is filling out across the stack, not iterating on the model. ChatGPT becoming an ad platform is the under-discussed shift here; it changes the incentive gradient on every product decision downstream. ### Open models keep landing — IBM Granite 4.1 and NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Nano Omni

Granite 4.1 is IBM's latest small-LLM line with a deep-dive on architecture and training choices. Nemotron 3 Nano Omni is a new multimodal model from NVIDIA targeting documents, audio, and video with extended context — "agent applications" is the explicit framing. Simon Willison ran 21 quantized variants of Granite 4.1 3B against SVG-generation prompts and posted results on Bluesky as a thread, useful as a real-world sanity check on what these models can actually do at small sizes. The pattern this week is open-weight releases targeting agent and edge use cases rather than chasing benchmarks. ### Apple to pay $250M over delayed Siri AI features

Class-action settlement covering AI capabilities Apple marketed as imminent but didn't ship on time. The $250M is a routine line item for Apple — but the case is the first major legal precedent on aggressive AI marketing claims. Other companies will read this and pull back: less "coming soon" in keynotes, more demo-on-the-day. The downstream effect on the AI roadmap-PR cycle is the real story, not the dollar figure — and it lands the same week OpenAI is publishing system cards on launch day. The signaling is converging. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/06/apple-to-pay-250m-to-settle-lawsuit-over-siris-delayed-ai-features/))

### Two essays from Simon Willison on agentic engineering

The first argues professional software engineering is converging with casual prompt-driven coding, eroding review and accountability practices — read in full as the practitioner signal of where this goes. The second is a writeup of an autonomous-agent restaurant experiment that turned into problems for actual suppliers and tax authorities, with Simon's takeaway that AI-run businesses "need to keep their own human operators in the loop" for actions affecting uninvolved third parties. Read together they're a useful counterweight to today's Cloudflare agent-deploy hype: the autonomy is real but the responsibility plumbing isn't.
