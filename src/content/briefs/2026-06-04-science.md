### GLP-1 drugs tied to lower addiction risk in 600,000-person veteran study

> **Summary.** Semaglutide and other GLP-1 *(glucagon-like peptide-1)* receptor agonists, the class including Ozempic and Wegovy, were associated with reduced incidence of substance use disorders and fewer overdoses across alcohol, nicotine, opioids, and stimulants. The retrospective cohort study of U.S. veterans is among the largest to date examining these off-metabolic effects. Researchers found lower rates of hospitalization, emergency visits, and drug-related deaths among those already struggling with addiction.

This isn't a randomized trial—it's observational, retrospective, and drawn from a predominantly male, older population that doesn't map cleanly onto broader demographics. The effect sizes look substantial, but GLP-1 users may differ systematically from non-users in ways the statistical adjustments can't fully capture: more medical contact, different socioeconomic markers, possible selection for health-consciousness. What's genuinely interesting is the *pattern* across substances—alcohol, nicotine, cannabis, cocaine, opioids all pointing the same direction—which hints at a shared neurobiological mechanism rather than a coincidence of confounding. The Nautilus companion piece on "food noise" *(intrusive preoccupation with eating)* quieting with GLP-1s offers a plausible framing: these drugs may dampen striatal reward salience broadly, not just for food. That's a testable hypothesis, and one that should move to controlled prospective studies rather than more database mining.

The clinical implication, if real, is enormous—addiction medicine has few scalable pharmacotherapies, especially for stimulant and alcohol use disorders. But we're not there yet. The VA population skews heavily male and white; sex differences in GLP-1 response for addiction are unexplored. And the study period captures early adoption, before the drugs became culturally ubiquitous, so secular trends may shift. We need a randomized trial with hard endpoints—overdose, sustained abstinence—before this changes practice. The momentum, though, is real.

### Experimental diabetes pill bypasses GLP-1 mechanism, activates muscle metabolism instead

> **Summary.** An oral compound activating ERRγ *(estrogen-related receptor gamma, a metabolic regulator)* in skeletal muscle showed early-phase safety and metabolic benefits in human testing: improved glucose control, increased fat oxidation, and preserved lean mass without the appetite suppression and gastrointestinal effects typical of GLP-1 drugs. The mechanism is orthogonal—targeting peripheral energy expenditure rather than central satiety signaling.

This is a genuine alternative pathway, and that's methodologically important for the field. GLP-1s dominate obesity pharmacotherapy, but tolerability drops many patients—nausea, vomiting, and the practical burden of injectables limit real-world adherence. An oral small-molecule with a different side-effect profile would expand the therapeutic map. The Phase 1 data are thin: the press release notes safety and tolerability without releasing actual numbers on weight loss magnitude, HbA1c changes, or comparator data. We don't know if the metabolic effects translate to clinically meaningful outcomes at scale. The muscle-sparing claim is particularly interesting given that GLP-1-associated lean mass loss is emerging as a concern, especially in older adults, but it's based on biomarkers and imaging in small cohorts, not functional preservation. The drug is years from market even with acceleration; the signal to watch is whether larger trials replicate the tolerability advantage without surprise liver or cardiac signals, which have sunk previous ERR-targeting programs.

### "Magic" in holographic physics linked to space-time's gravitational flexibility

> **Summary.** Quantum "magic" *(a measure of non-stabilizerness—how far a quantum state is from the efficiently simulable class of stabilizer states)* has been proposed as the quantity governing how pliable emergent space-time is in holographic theories. Researchers argue that higher magic correlates with stronger gravitational effects in the bulk, suggesting a concrete quantum-information property that could underlie Einstein's equations.

This is methodology-shifting for the AdS/CFT *(anti-de Sitter/conformal field theory correspondence, the holographic principle's most precise realization)* program, which has struggled to connect abstract entanglement measures to the specific dynamics of gravity. Entanglement entropy built space-time's connectivity; "magic" now appears to build its elasticity—how it responds to stress and curves. The evidence is theoretical, built on tensor network models and specific holographic codes rather than empirical observation, but it's a sharply posed conjecture with concrete predictions. The framing in Quanta is appropriately cautious: this is a "may have traced," not a "discovered." What's strong is the explanatory compression—two quantum-information concepts, entanglement and magic, potentially yielding both the existence and the dynamics of gravity. What's thin is the distance to any experimental test; we're not measuring magic in the lab, and the relevant holographic duals don't describe our universe directly. Still, for a field that has historically borrowed mathematics from gravity to understand quantum systems, the reverse direction—quantum information reconstructing gravity's details—is the program's deepest aspiration.

([Quanta Magazine](https://www.quantamagazine.org/entanglement-builds-space-time-now-magic-gives-it-gravity-20260603/))
