### Lebanon Buffer, Revisited

> **Summary.** *The Dispatch* makes the case for a formal Israeli security zone in southern Lebanon as the fastest way to degrade Hezbollah's recovery capacity. The argument assumes military control can succeed where fifteen years of UN peacekeeping *(UNIFIL, ineffectual since 2006)* failed.

The piece treats Hezbollah's post-October 2023 degradation as durable fact, but the evidence there is thinner than the framing suggests. The group has lost senior commanders and much of its rocket infrastructure, yes, yet it retains recruitment networks, Iranian resupply, and the lesson that extended Israeli occupation breeds resistance. The security-zone proposal isn't novel—it's what Israel attempted in the 1980s-90s, and Hezbollah was born from that experience. The article's strength is its honesty about timelines: it admits this would require years, not months, and acknowledges the risk of mission creep. What's missing is any engagement with why Hezbollah might not simply wait out the occupation, or how a buffer zone differs strategically from the failed 2006 settlement.

The domestic Israeli politics get short shrift. Netanyahu's coalition needs a win; a "security zone" is saleable as one. That doesn't make the military case wrong, but it explains why this idea is circulating now. Treat it as policy argument with incentives attached, not detached strategic analysis.

([The Dispatch](https://thedispatch.com/article/israel-hezbollah-lebanon-security-zone/))

### Europe's Resilience, Overstated?

> **Summary.** A contrary take on European decline narratives: *The Dispatch* argues the EU is strengthening institutionally, with Franco-German cooperation reviving and the UK quietly re-engaging.

The evidence here is genuinely mixed, which the piece acknowledges more than it resolves. Macron's Germany trip yielded actual defense spending commitments—unusual for Franco-German summits, which typically produce communiqué theater. The UK-EU defense cooperation framework is real but thin; it's not membership, and Labour's domestic constraints limit how far Starmer can go. The stronger case is internal: Hungary's isolation on Ukraine policy, the suspension of EU funds to Budapest, and the European Council's new qualified-majority voting on sanctions. These are institutional developments that matter.

What's overstated is "strength" as unity. The article concedes Germany's coalition collapse and Poland's ongoing rule-of-law disputes in passing, then returns to the upward narrative. Europe is more capable than the "crisis" framing allows, but "stronger than critics predicted" sets a low bar. The better read: Europe is muddling through with institutional tools that finally work, occasionally, which is different from coherence.

([The Dispatch](https://thedispatch.com/article/europe-hungary-european-union-united-kingdom-france-germany/))

### RFK Jr.'s Psychiatric Policy Shift

> **Summary.** HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. announced federal steps to reduce antidepressant prescribing and support medication discontinuation. The framing is "overmedicalization"; the mechanism remains vague.

The policy substance here is less dramatic than the headlines suggest. Kennedy's May 4 event promised "guidance" and "alternative treatment options," not immediate regulatory change. What's notable is the convergence: a Trump appointtee using progressive-sounding language about psychiatric overreach, while progressive critics note Kennedy's long history of vaccine skepticism and disputed claims about SSRIs causing violence. The *AllSides* cluster tracks this as health news, but it's properly political—an alliance of convenience between wellness-left "de-prescribing" advocates and right-wing distrust of institutional medicine.

The evidentiary gap is wide. Kennedy cited no new studies; the research on SSRI efficacy is contested but does not support blanket de-prescribing. The "discontinuation support" pledge is the interesting piece—if HHS funds actual tapering protocols, that addresses a real gap in American psychiatry. But the framing suggests causation (overprescription as error) where the data shows correlation (high prescription rates in a system with poor access to therapy). Watch whether this produces funded services or merely messaging.
