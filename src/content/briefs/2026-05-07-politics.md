### The Final Lebanon War?

> **Summary.** Israeli military planners are considering a formal security zone in southern Lebanon to accelerate Hezbollah's degradation beyond its recovery capacity. The proposal frames occupation not as indefinite nation-building but as a calibrated pressure mechanism.

The Dispatch's analysis treats southern Lebanon as a test case for what Israel calls "mowing the grass" — limited, repeatable campaigns that deny adversaries regeneration time. The article's strength is its direct engagement with Israeli strategic logic: a security zone creates friction for Hezbollah's resupply while avoiding the attritional trap of the 1982-2000 occupation. What's thinner is the counterfactual — whether Hezbollah's Iranian backing makes regeneration rates structurally immune to territorial denial. The piece doesn't model costs to Israel: international legitimacy erosion, casualty tolerance in an ongoing Gaza war, or the Lebanese state's potential collapse. The framing "Final Lebanon War" is aspirational, not predictive — wars labeled final have a habit of proving otherwise.

The policy question underneath: can Israel define victory conditions narrowly enough to exit, or does a security zone become self-perpetuating the moment Hezbollah fires from beyond it? The article assumes Israeli decision-making coherence that recent cabinet dynamics don't fully support.

([The Dispatch](https://thedispatch.com/article/israel-hezbollah-lebanon-security-zone/))

### RFK Jr.'s Psychiatric Pivot

> **Summary.** HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. announced federal initiatives to reduce psychiatric medication prescribing and expand non-pharmaceutical mental health interventions. The plan frames "overmedicalization" as a policy failure requiring systematic correction.

AllSides' aggregation captures the announcement's breadth: Kennedy's HHS is positioning antidepressant *(SSRIs and similar)* reduction as a population-health priority, with unspecified "alternative treatments" as the replacement. The sourcing here is entirely administrative — Kennedy's May 4 remarks and HHS press materials. What's absent is clinical input, implementation detail, or the inevitable CMS *(Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services)* rulemaking that would actually change prescribing incentives. The framing of "weaning" patients off medications carries explicit stigma risk; the article notes Kennedy's long-standing vaccine skepticism but doesn't connect it to institutional trust questions this initiative will face. Stronger reporting would examine whether this represents genuine care-model experimentation or ideological preference dressed as evidence-based reform. The signal to watch: FDA guidance changes or CMS reimbursement shifts that would make this operational rather than rhetorical.

### North Korea's Unification Divorce

> **Summary.** Constitutional revisions remove all references to Korean reunification, formalizing Pyongyang's "two hostile states" doctrine and defining Kim Jong Un's explicit authority over nuclear weapons.

The constitutional change is procedural confirmation of rhetorical shifts dating to 2023, but its timing matters. Kim's 2024 abandonment of reunification as policy goal now has foundational legal status, with territorial definitions that treat the DMZ as permanent border rather than temporary division. The AllSides aggregation captures expert disagreement: some read this as normalization toward state-to-state diplomacy, others as clearing legal obstacles for tactical nuclear use by removing pan-Korean identity constraints. The sourcing mix — Yonhap, Newsweek, Chosun Daily — surfaces this interpretive split without resolving it. What the coverage underweights: constitutional text in North Korea functions as performative instrument, not constraint. The stronger signal is military posture change south of Pyongyang; the weaker signal is whether this enables any diplomatic flexibility or merely entrenches confrontation.
