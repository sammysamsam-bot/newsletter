### A Meniscus Surgery That Probably Shouldn't Happen

> **Summary.** A decade-long Finnish trial found that arthroscopic partial meniscectomy — trimming a torn knee meniscus — offered no benefit over placebo surgery, with treated patients actually showing worse outcomes at 10 years: more pain, faster osteoarthritis progression, and higher rates of reoperation. The finding directly contradicts a procedure performed roughly 750,000 times annually in the US alone.

The evidence here is unusually strong for surgical orthopedics: a randomized controlled trial with a legitimate sham-surgery control group, 146 patients, and follow-up stretching past the typical 1-2 year window where placebo effects dominate. The FIDELITY trial's 10-year data show treated knees deteriorated faster on every measured dimension — symptoms, function, cartilage loss, and conversion to knee replacement. The mechanism isn't mysterious: removing meniscal tissue alters joint loading in ways that accelerate degenerative change. What's striking is how long the field persisted with this procedure despite earlier warning signals from smaller trials. The American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons actually downgraded its recommendation in 2013, yet utilization barely budged. This is where practice change gets political: surgeon training, patient demand, and reimbursement structures all resist retractions. The honest caveat is that FIDELITY enrolled patients with degenerative meniscal tears without acute mechanical locking — traumatic tears in younger patients weren't studied, and the trial's exclusion criteria matter for generalization.

The broader pattern is more important than this single procedure. Orthopedic surgery has accumulated multiple high-profile reversals — vertebroplasty, rotator cuff decompression, now this — where sham-controlled trials deflate interventions that seemed obviously beneficial. The field's relative openness to placebo controls is admirable; its sluggishness in implementing findings less so. For patients, the actionable takeaway is specific: if you're over 35 with knee pain and an MRI showing meniscal wear, the person recommending trimming should explain why this trial doesn't apply to you. Often it won't, and that's worth naming explicitly.

### Nine Weeks of Pembrolizumab Before Surgery Eradicates Colon Cancer in Trial

> **Summary.** A UK-led trial of neoadjuvant pembrolizumab *(anti-PD-1 immunotherapy, Merck's Keytruda)* — nine weeks of treatment before surgical resection — produced disease-free survival of roughly 95% at nearly three years for mismatch repair-deficient colorectal cancer. The control arm receiving standard chemo followed by surgery showed comparable results, but with substantially more toxicity.

The trial design is what makes this practice-shifting. Standard of care for localized mismatch repair-deficient (dMMR) colon cancer is surgery first, then risk-stratified adjuvant therapy. The NICHE-2 and subsequent studies flipped the sequence, exploiting immunotherapy's particular efficacy in tumors with high mutation burden. What's new here is the durability signal: median follow-up of 35 months with sustained remissions, and a treatment burden — nine weeks, no chemotherapy — that dramatically outperforms the months of adjuvant 5-FU typically required. The limitation is implicit in the trial population: these are dMMR tumors, roughly 15% of colorectal cancers, already known to be immunotherapy-responsive. The framing as "eradication" requires qualification — these are pathological complete response rates in resected tissue, not documented cure, though the clinical trajectory suggests functional cure in many cases. The comparison to standard chemotherapy is genuinely favorable on toxicity; whether it's superior on cancer control will require longer follow-up and larger cohorts.

This fits a pattern in oncology where neoadjuvant immunotherapy is resequencing treatment for multiple tumor types. The methodological strength is real — prospective trial, central pathology review, standardized surgical approach — but the sample (103 treated) is modest and the control historical rather than randomized. The FDA granted breakthrough designation for this indication in 2024; these data support conversion to full approval. For clinicians, the practical question is whether community hospitals can replicate the multidisciplinary coordination (oncology-surgery-pathology turnaround) that makes short-course neoadjuvant therapy feasible. For patients with dMMR tumors, this increasingly looks like the new standard.

### Lightning Physics: The Old Mystery Gets New Instruments

> **Summary.** Recent atmospheric measurements using *LOFAR* *(Low-Frequency Array, Dutch-led radio telescope network)* and airborne sensors are revealing storm clouds as sites of violent, previously unobserved electrical phenomena — including "fast breakdown" events and stepped leader formation at resolutions impossible until recently.

The Quanta framing — "the answer keeps getting more interesting" — understates the methodological shift. Lightning initiation has been understood in broad strokes since the 1920s: charge separation in convective cells, leader propagation, return stroke. What remained mysterious was the *initiation* problem: how fields build to breakdown strength in regions where conventional calculations suggest they shouldn't. The new data from LOFAR's lightning-mapping capabilities and recent airborne campaigns show streamer formation preceding leaders, energetic "precursor" events, and spatial structures that challenge the standard bi-directional leader model. The evidence quality is high for observational geophysics: instrument-verified, reproducible across storm systems, with physical mechanisms testable in laboratory discharge chambers. What's thin is the integration — these are phenomena catalogued more than explained, and the theoretical framework for how storms generate the observed field enhancements remains contested.

The honest hedge is that "closing in" depends on your definition. We have better movies of lightning formation now, and the phenomenology is richer than textbooks suggested. Whether this constitutes progress toward *prediction* — of stroke location, intensity, initiation timing — is less clear. The research program is valuable regardless: understanding how charged precipitation, graupel, and ice crystal populations interact to produce breakdown conditions has implications beyond meteorology, including for aviation and power-grid protection. The instruments are the story here. LOFAR was built for radio astronomy; its repurposing for atmospheric physics exemplifies how infrastructure investment in one domain enables unexpected advances elsewhere.
